In the life cycle of every registered company in Ghana, corporate change is an inevitable reality.

Founders step down to pursue new ventures, institutional investors appoint non-executive directors to board seats, trusted colleagues pass away, tenures expire under corporate constitutions, and uncommitted officers sometimes abandon their operational posts.

However, many Ghanaian business owners treat a board or secretarial change as an internal office arrangement that concludes once a resignation email is sent or a board meeting adjourns.

Under the **Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992)**, that assumption is legally perilous.

A change in the leadership or governance structure of a company is not legally complete until it is officially registered with the **Office of the Registrar of Companies (ORC)**. Until the statutory instruments are processed and the public register is amended, the old board structure remains the only legal authority recognized by the High Court of Ghana, commercial banks, the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA), and institutional counterparties.

When an outgoing director is left on the public register, they carry lingering personal liability for company debts they had no hand in incurring. Conversely, when an incoming managing director is omitted from the register, commercial banks will refuse to honor their signature on corporate accounts.

This guide provides the definitive, plain-English roadmap for executing a **Change of Director or Company Secretary** in Ghana: detailing the 28-day statutory deadline, analyzing the four distinct grounds for change, calculating official ORC fees, and outlining the exact documentary evidence required.

## 1. The Statutory Duty of Notification: The 28-Day Rule

Under Section 176 (for Directors) and Section 211 (for Secretaries) of Act 992, the Republic of Ghana imposes a strict statutory notification timeline on every registered corporate body:

Whenever a company appoints a new director, accepts a resignation, effects a removal, or records the death of an officer—or whenever an existing officer changes their legal name, residential address, nationality, or marital status—the company **must formally deliver notice of the change to the Registrar of Companies within twenty-eight (28) days**.

### The Statutory Filing Instruments
- **Form 17:** *Notification of Change of Directors or Particulars of Directors*.
- **Form 17A:** *Notification of Change of Company Secretary or Particulars of Company Secretary*.

### The Danger of Non-Compliance
Failing to file within the statutory 28-day window triggers two serious liabilities:
1. **Accrued Default Penalties:** The company and every officer in default are liable to pay administrative penalty fines to the ORC for each day the default continues.
2. **Third-Party Fiduciary Estoppel:** Under Ghanaian corporate jurisprudence, third parties dealing with the company are entitled to rely on the ORC public register. If an outgoing director signs a contract or incurs a corporate liability before Form 17 is filed, the company may remain legally bound by their actions.

> "A resignation letter in your corporate filing cabinet protects nobody. Until Form 17 is stamped by the Registrar of Companies, the law regards you as fully responsible for every action of the board."

## 2. The Four Grounds for Change & Mandatory Evidence

Under Act 992, the ORC does not process board changes on verbal assertions. The documentation required varies strictly depending on the **specific factual and legal reason** for the change:

### Scenario 1: Resignation of a Director or Secretary
When an officer voluntarily steps down from office before the expiration of their term:
- ✓ **Formal Resignation Letter:** A signed letter addressed to the Board of Directors, specifying the clear intent to resign and stating the precise effective date.
- ✓ **Company Written Resolution:** A formal board or shareholder resolution acknowledging, accepting, and ratifying the resignation.
- ✓ **Duly Executed Form 17 or 17A:** Completed by the Company Secretary or surviving director, detailing the date of cessation.
- ✓ **Replacement Consent (If Appointing a Successor):** If a new officer is appointed simultaneously, attach their signed Consent Letter, Statutory Declaration witnessed by a Commissioner for Oaths, and separate front-and-back scans of their valid Ghana Card.

### Scenario 2: Removal Due to Death
When a serving director or secretary passes away:
- ✓ **Certified Proof of Death:** An official **Death Certificate** issued by the Births and Deaths Registry of Ghana, or a certified Coroner's Report / Medical Certificate of Cause of Death.
- ✓ **Company Written Resolution:** A board or shareholder resolution officially recording the passing, declaring the office vacant, and (if necessary) appointing an interim or permanent successor.
- ✓ **Duly Executed Form 17 or 17A:** Stating the date of death as the effective date of cessation.

### Scenario 3: Removal Due to End of Term / Tenure Expiration
When an officer’s appointment expires by operation of the company’s Constitution or terms of appointment:
- ✓ **Company Written Resolution:** An Ordinary Resolution passed at an Annual General Meeting (AGM) or Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM), or a board resolution confirming that the officer's tenure has concluded without renewal.
- ✓ **Constitutional Reference:** Documentation citing the specific section of the Company Regulations or employment agreement establishing the term ceiling.
- ✓ **Duly Executed Form 17 or 17A:** Confirming the date of expiration.

### Scenario 4: Removal Due to Abandonment of Office / Post
One of the most complex corporate governance dilemmas occurs when a director or secretary ceases to attend meetings, abdicates their fiduciary responsibilities, or becomes completely untraceable without submitting a resignation:
- ✓ **Written Evidence of Demand Letters:** Copies of formal registered warning or reference letters sent to the officer’s last known residential address and corporate email, demanding that they resume duties or show cause within a specified statutory notice period.
- ✓ **Proof of Delivery / Failed Delivery:** Courier receipts, registered post vouchers, or delivery confirmations establishing that reasonable diligence was exercised to notify the officer.
- ✓ **Company Written Resolution:** A detailed board or shareholder resolution citing abandonment of post, detailing the officer's prolonged absence, and formally resolving to remove them from office under Section 176 of Act 992 and the Company Constitution.
- ✓ **Duly Executed Form 17 or 17A:** Documenting removal for post abandonment.

## 3. The 5-Step Official ORC Filing Procedure

Filing a change of director or secretary with the Office of the Registrar of Companies follows a structured administrative workflow:

1. **Step 1 — Draft & Execute Corporate Resolutions:** Convene the board or shareholders to pass the appropriate Ordinary or Board Written Resolution detailing the exact nature of the change (resignation, removal, or appointment).
2. **Step 2 — Complete Form 17 (Director) or Form 17A (Secretary):** Accurately fill out the official statutory notification form, specifying the company's full name, registration number, date of change, outgoing officer's details, and incoming officer's full biographical particulars.
3. **Step 3 — Assemble Identity & Consent Documents for New Officers:** If appointing a new director, ensure they sign the mandatory **Consent Letter** and **Statutory Declaration** witnessed by a Commissioner for Oaths or Notary Public. Compile their GhanaPost GPS digital address, TIN, mother's maiden name, and separate front-and-back scans of their valid Ghana Card.
4. **Step 4 — Settle Statutory Filing Fees at the In-House Bank:** Submit the complete dossier at the ORC amendments counter and settle official statutory fees:
   - **Form 17 / Form 17A Filing Fee:** **GHS 60** per instrument.
   - **Certified True Copy (CTC) Fee:** **GHS 30** per instrument.
5. **Step 5 — Examination, Approval & Issuance of Stamped CTC:** The Registrar audits the submission against Act 992 compliance. Upon approval, the ORC updates the national database and issues the official **stamped Certified True Copy (CTC) of Form 17 or 17A**.

> "A filing is not complete until you hold the stamped Certified True Copy (CTC) of Form 17 in your hands. That CTC is your legal shield against bank freezes and regulatory disputes."

## 4. The Ripple Effect: Updating Commercial Banks, GRA, and Regulators

Filing Form 17 with the ORC is the primary legal milestone. However, an enterprise must immediately translate this update across its institutional relationships:

### 1. Updating Commercial Bank Signatory Mandates
Commercial banks in Ghana are subject to stringent Bank of Ghana Anti-Money Laundering (AML) directives. 
- When an executive director steps down, the bank **will not remove their signatory authority or recognize a new signatory** until the company delivers an official, stamped **Certified True Copy (CTC) of Form 17** directly from the ORC.
- Delaying Form 17 filing freezes corporate accounts, halts payroll disbursements, and disrupts supplier wires.

### 2. Updating the Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) Tax Portal
Under modern tax administration in Ghana, company director profiles are digitally linked to the company’s Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN).
- When a director changes, the company must submit the stamped Form 17 CTC to its local GRA Taxpayer Service Centre to update the corporate tax ledger.
- This ensures that personal tax liabilities are not erroneously assessed against outgoing directors, and allows incoming directors to secure valid Tax Clearance Certificates (TCC).

### 3. Updating Sectoral Regulators
For regulated enterprises, board changes require immediate notification to statutory supervisory bodies:
- Financial Institutions: Bank of Ghana (BoG).
- Capital Markets: Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).
- Insurance Providers: National Insurance Commission (NIC).
- Foreign-Invested Companies: Ghana Investment Promotion Centre (GIPC).

## 5. Critical Traps to Avoid During Board Changes

In our corporate advisory practice, we routinely rescue companies entangled in administrative paralysis caused by basic governance blunders:

- **1.01** **Falling Below the Statutory Director Floor:** A private company must maintain at least one director (Act 992 Section 170), while a public company must maintain at least two. If a director resigns and leaves the board vacant, the company is in immediate statutory breach and cannot execute lawful corporate acts.
- **1.02** **The Director-Secretary Conflict Trap:** Under Section 211(5), a sole director cannot also serve as Company Secretary. If your co-director resigns and leaves you as sole director, you cannot appoint yourself as secretary; you must engage a separate qualified professional.
- **1.03** **Omission of Mother's Maiden Name:** On Ghanaian statutory forms, the mother's maiden name is a mandatory KYC security identifier. Submitting Form 17 without this data results in immediate counter rejection.
- **1.04** **Exceeding the 28-Day Deadline:** Delaying submission beyond 28 days accumulates statutory penalty fines that must be cleared before the ORC will issue your Certified True Copies.
