When enterprise ambitions outgrow the boundaries of private syndicates, family balance sheets, and bilateral bank facilities, the modern corporation must graduate to the premier legal architecture in Ghanaian commerce: **The Public Company Limited by Shares (PLC)**.

Governed by the Office of the Registrar of Companies (ORC) under the **Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992)**, a Public Company is purpose-built for enterprises designed to mobilize capital at scale.

Whether orchestrating an initial public offering (IPO) on the Ghana Stock Exchange (GSE), establishing a licensed commercial bank or non-bank financial institution under Bank of Ghana guidelines, launching a nationwide industrial manufacturing plant, or issuing publicly traded corporate debt instruments, the PLC structure provides the ultimate vehicle for commercial expansion.

Under Act 992, the public corporation is recognized as an institutional pillar of the economy. In exchange for the rare statutory privilege of raising capital directly from the public, the Republic demands the highest standards of corporate governance, fiduciary duty, capital transparency, and independent auditing.

For executive founders, corporate counsel, institutional investment syndicates, and diaspora consortiums, understanding the mechanics of **Form 3C**, statutory stated capital requirements, auditor rotation mandates, and director onboarding is essential.

This guide provides the definitive, plain-English roadmap for incorporating a Public Company Limited by Shares in Ghana in 2026.

## 1. The Anatomy of a Public Company (PLC): Characteristics & Purpose

Under Section 7 and Section 21 of the Companies Act, 2019 (Act 992), a Public Company Limited by Shares is defined by distinct legal characteristics that separate it from all other corporate formations:

### Core Structural Features
- ✓ **Public Capital Mobilization:** Unlike private companies, a PLC possesses the unrestricted statutory right to invite the general public to subscribe for its shares, debentures, and debt securities (subject to SEC clearance).
- ✓ **Free Transferability of Shares:** The corporate constitution of a PLC cannot restrict the transfer of its shares, enabling liquidity, secondary market trading, and capital access.
- ✓ **Unlimited Shareholder Capacity:** While a private company (Ltd) is capped at a maximum of 50 shareholders, a PLC can accommodate thousands of retail and institutional shareholders worldwide.
- ✓ **Limited Liability Protection:** The liability of each shareholder is strictly limited to the amount, if any, unpaid on the shares they hold. Once shares are fully paid up, personal liability ceases.
- ✓ **Perpetual Succession:** The death, bankruptcy, resignation, or retirement of any shareholder or director does not affect the continuous legal existence of the corporate entity.
- ✓ **Mandatory "PLC" Statutory Suffix:** Under Section 21(1)(a) of Act 992, every public company limited by shares must bear the word **"Public Limited Company"** or the official abbreviation **"PLC"** as the final element of its registered corporate name (e.g., *Ghana Infrastructure Holdings PLC*).

### Commercial Applications
A Public Company is not designed for micro-enterprises. It is the mandatory or optimal legal structure for:
- Large-scale manufacturing, industrial, and processing conglomerates.
- Commercial banks, specialized deposit-taking institutions, and insurance underwriters.
- Mining, petroleum, and energy infrastructure consortiums.
- Companies preparing for listing on the Ghana Stock Exchange (GSE) or the Ghana Alternative Market (GAX).
- Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) and public collective investment schemes.

> "A Private Company Limited by Shares is built to protect personal equity; a Public Company Limited by Shares (PLC) is engineered to absorb, deploy, and compound public capital."

## 2. The Corporate Spectrum: PLC vs. Ltd vs. Limited by Guarantee

To avoid critical formation errors, founders and promoters must distinguish between the three primary corporate formations available under Act 992:

- **1.01** **Public Company Limited by Shares (PLC — Form 3C):** Formed for commercial profit. Features share capital, unrestricted share transfers, unlimited shareholders, public capital solicitation rights, and a mandatory minimum of two directors.
- **1.02** **Private Company Limited by Shares (Ltd — Form 3A):** Formed for commercial profit. Features share capital capped at 50 shareholders, restricts share transfers, prohibits public offerings, and allows a minimum of one director.
- **1.03** **Company Limited by Guarantee (Form 3B):** Formed strictly for non-profit and non-commercial purposes (NGOs, Foundations, Charities, Churches, Ministries, Research Institutes, and Trade Associations). **It has no share capital.** Members act as guarantors rather than shareholders, pledging a nominal sum in the event of winding up. Under Act 992, a Company Limited by Guarantee is strictly prohibited from paying dividends or distributing profits to its members.

## 3. The 12-Step Official ORC Registration Procedure (Form 3C)

Incorporating a Public Company Limited by Shares with the Office of the Registrar of Companies requires executing a rigorous, 12-step statutory workflow:

1. **Step 1 — Official Name Availability Search & Reservation:** Conduct a formal search via the ORC portal to verify that the proposed corporate name is unique, available, and not deceptively similar to an existing entity. The name must conclude with the statutory abbreviation **"PLC"**. Approved names can be reserved for 30 days.
2. **Step 2 — Complete the Public Company Registration Form (Form 3C):** Prepare the comprehensive **Form 3C** (Application for Incorporation of Public Company Limited by Shares), detailing corporate objects, authorized share numbers, classes of shares, registered office address, and principal place of business.
3. **Step 3 — Secure ICAG Auditor Consent Letter & 6-Year Rotation Undertaking:** Provide a formal written consent letter and statutory declaration from a practicing Chartered Accountant or audit firm licensed by the **Institute of Chartered Accountants Ghana (ICAG)**. Under Section 139 of Act 992, the auditor is eligible to serve for a maximum statutory term of **six (6) consecutive years**, after which the company is legally required to rotate to a new external audit firm.
4. **Step 4 — Swear Statutory Declarations Before a Commissioner for Oaths:** All statutory declarations embedded within Form 3C and director schedules must be sworn and stamped by a Commissioner for Oaths, Notary Public, or self-declared under statutory oath.
5. **Step 5 — Structure Stated Capital & Share Classes:** Formalize the company's stated capital structure, defining authorized share volumes, issued shares, consideration paid (cash or property other than cash), and class rights (ordinary vs. preference shares).
6. **Step 6 — Board & Secretarial Consent Execution:** Under Section 170 of Act 992, appoint a **minimum of two (2) directors**. Each director must execute an individual Statutory Declaration and Consent Letter affirming their eligibility and mental capacity. The appointed **Company Secretary** must also submit a formal consent letter and evidence of professional qualification.
7. **Step 7 — Beneficial Ownership (BO) Disclosure:** In full compliance with national anti-money laundering (AML) and Financial Action Task Force (FATF) protocols, complete the mandatory Beneficial Ownership disclosure schedules for every individual who directly or indirectly holds 5% or 10% voting equity or exercises ultimate operational control.
8. **Step 8 — Counter Submission for Auditing & Data Capturing:** Submit all completed, sworn, and signed documents at the ORC Public Company Registration Counter for physical verification, document scrutiny, and data capturing into the national registry.
9. **Step 9 — Settle Base Statutory Fees (GHS 585 + 1% Capital Duty):** Pay the official statutory processing and incorporation fee of **GHS 585** at the ORC in-house bank. Pay the mandatory **1% Capital Duty** calculated on the company's declared stated capital.
10. **Step 10 — Optional VIP / Prestige Fast-Track Processing (GHS 1,885):** For executive teams operating under strict transactional timelines, the ORC offers a VIP Prestige Service. This carries an additional statutory fee of **GHS 1,885** (payable at the in-house bank alongside the base fee), ensuring expedited review and issuance within 24 to 48 business hours.
11. **Step 11 — Official Examination, Approval, and Document Issuance:** Upon thorough statutory review by the Registrar, the ORC issues four foundational legal instruments:
    - The official **Certificate of Incorporation** (with unique Corporate Registration Number).
    - The **Certified True Copy (CTC) of the Registered Constitution of the Company**.
    - The **Certified True Copy (CTC) of Form 3C**.
    - The official **Beneficial Ownership (BO) Registration Profiles**.
12. **Step 12 — Mandatory Annual Returns (GHS 175 + Audited Accounts):** Under Section 126 of Act 992, every PLC must file its first Annual Return with the ORC within **18 months of incorporation**, accompanied by full **Audited Financial Statements** signed by its ICAG-licensed auditor. Subsequent Annual Returns must be filed every 12 months (within 42 days of the balance sheet date) at an official fee of **GHS 175**.

> "Under Act 992, corporate auditing is not a ceremonial rubber stamp. The mandatory 6-year ICAG auditor rotation rule ensures institutional transparency, prevents conflicts of interest, and safeguards public shareholder capital."

## 4. The Master Client & Institutional Intake Checklist

To execute a flawless Form 3C incorporation without procedural delays or counter rejections, promoters must compile comprehensive particulars across all participating officers:

### Category A: Corporate Identity & Objects
- **3 Proposed Company Names (ending in PLC):** Ranked in strict order of priority (e.g., *1. Apex Industrial Manufacturing PLC*, *2. Apex Processing Ghana PLC*, *3. Apex Agro-Industrial Group PLC*).
- **Principal Business Activities / Objects:** A comprehensive description of the industrial, commercial, and financial activities the company will execute.
- **Stated Capital & Initial Share Allocation:** Declared cash capital, number of ordinary shares issued, price per share, and share register breakdown.

### Category B: First Director Particulars (Mandatory)
- **GhanaPost GPS Digital Address:** Mandatory 11-digit national digital address of residential residence (e.g., *GA-102-4932*).
- **Residential House & Street Details:** House/plot number, street name, permanent landmark, town/city, district, and region.
- **Postal & Contact Particulars:** P.O. Box / PMB, primary telephone number, and official personal email address.
- **Biographical KYC Details:** Full legal first name, surname, other names, date of birth, place of birth, marital status, and primary occupation.
- **Tax Identification:** Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) linked to Ghana Card.
- **Maternal Verification:** Mother’s maiden name / full name (statutory KYC security data).
- **National Identity Document:** Clear, high-resolution digital scans of the **Front and Back of a valid Ghana Card, captured separately**.

### Category C: Second Director Particulars (Mandatory)
Under Act 992 Section 170, a PLC **must** have a minimum of two directors at all times:
- **Identical Comprehensive Profile:** Submit the complete, separate set of records for the Second Director, including GhanaPost GPS digital address, residential details, contact channels, full biographical data, TIN, mother's maiden name, and separate front-and-back scans of their valid Ghana Card.

### Category D: Company Secretary Particulars (Mandatory)
Under Section 211 of Act 992, a Company Secretary in a public company must possess verified statutory qualifications (a barrister, chartered accountant, or chartered secretary):
- **Full Secretarial Profile:** Complete residential GPS address, house/street identifiers, contact lines, biographical particulars, TIN, mother's maiden name, and separate front-and-back Ghana Card scans.
- **Secretarial Consent Letter:** Duly signed letter agreeing to serve as Company Secretary, accompanied by professional practicing certificate numbers where applicable.

### Category E: Statutory Auditor Particulars (Mandatory)
- **Audit Firm Profile:** Official registered name of the accounting firm (operating as a licensed Sole Proprietorship or Partnership with ICAG).
- **ICAG License Number:** Valid firm practice certificate number issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants Ghana.
- **Auditor Consent & Declaration:** Formal written consent on audit firm letterhead confirming willingness to act as statutory auditor under Act 992 provisions.

## 5. Strict Governance Under Act 992: Directors, Secretaries, and Auditors

The Companies Act, 2019 introduced uncompromising corporate governance provisions that hold officers of a PLC personally and criminally liable for fiduciary breaches:

### Director Eligibility & Disqualifications (Section 173)
A person is automatically disqualified from serving as a director of a Public Company if they:
- Have been convicted of an offence involving fraud, dishonesty, or breach of fiduciary duty within the preceding five years.
- Are an undischarged bankrupt.
- Are deemed of unsound mind by a court of competent jurisdiction.
- Have been barred by the High Court under a disqualification order.

### The Role of the Company Secretary (Section 211)
In a Public Company, the Company Secretary is not a clerical note-taker; they are the chief legal compliance and governance officer of the board. They are legally tasked with:
- Ensuring board resolutions comply with Act 992 and the Company Constitution.
- Maintaining statutory registers of members, directors, charges, and beneficial owners.
- Directing the timely filing of Annual Returns, [board and secretarial changes via Form 17/17A](/insights/how-to-change-company-director-secretary-ghana-orc-form-17), and financial disclosures to the ORC.

### Auditor Independence & The 6-Year Rotation Rule (Section 139)
To eradicate long-standing corporate complacency, Act 992 revolutionized external auditing in Ghana. An auditor of a public company:
- Cannot be an officer, director, or partner of an officer of the company.
- Cannot hold shares in the corporate entity.
- **Cannot serve for more than six (6) continuous years.** Once the 6-year ceiling is reached, the audit firm must step down and cannot be reappointed until a cooling-off period elapses.

## 6. The Post-Incorporation Roadmap: Capital Markets & Regulatory Approvals

Securing your Certificate of Incorporation from the ORC establishes your legal birth as a public corporation. 

However, before a PLC can actively solicit funds or execute licensed operations, it must navigate the secondary regulatory layer:

- **1.01** **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Approval:** Under the Securities Industry Act, 2016 (Act 929), any public invitation to purchase shares, corporate bonds, or mutual fund units requires the submission, review, and approval of a formal **Prospectus** by the SEC.
- **1.02** **Ghana Stock Exchange (GSE) Listing:** If the public company plans to list its equities for trading on the main board or the Ghana Alternative Market (GAX), it must fulfill minimum stated capital requirements, public float percentages (minimum 25% public ownership), and 3-year audited track records.
- **1.03** **Sectoral Operating Licenses:** Depending on its corporate objects, a PLC must secure secondary operational licenses from the **Bank of Ghana (BoG)** for banking/fintech, **National Insurance Commission (NIC)** for insurance, **National Communications Authority (NCA)** for telecoms, or the **Petroleum and Minerals Commissions** for extractive industries.
- **1.04** **Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) Large Taxpayer Office (LTO):** PLCs are typically assigned to the GRA's specialized Large Taxpayer Office, requiring full compliance with electronic VAT invoicing (e-VAT), Corporate Income Tax (CIT) quarterly self-assessments, and cross-border transfer pricing documentation.
